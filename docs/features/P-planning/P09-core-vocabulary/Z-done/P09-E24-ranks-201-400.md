# P09-E24. Ranks 201–400 — the same coverage check, one band further

**Feature:** rerun P09's coverage check for COCA lemma ranks 201–400, and turn what is missing into
the same kind of checklist P09 §2 and §3 were.
**Shape:** a research task. Its output is a checklist in this file, then seed tickets and construct
task files filed from it — no code.
**Scope:** English checklist, all 7 languages for whatever it asks to seed (as P09).
**Status:** **done 2026-09-24.** Filed 2026-09-23 from P09's follow-ups
([README *Follow-ups*](../README.md#follow-ups), *Ranks 201–400*). The checklist is in
[*Done*](#done); its seed rows are B75–B90 and its constructs P09-E25–E43.

**The word list is not in this file, and must not be written from memory.** Ranks past 150 shuffle
between corpora and editions (P09 *Why*), so a recalled list would be wrong in ways nobody could
check. The list is **to be fetched and verified** as step 1.

## Why

P09 took the top 200 from "335 concepts, many of them grammar terms" to every word seeded or built.
The next 200 are still everyday vocabulary, and they are the cheapest measurable next step: the
method exists, the source is fixed, and the corpus has doubled since the first check.

## Design

### D1. Fetch the list, record its provenance, keep one source

**Recommendation:** download the COCA lemma list (any edition that reaches rank 400; what is
freely available and under which terms is itself to be checked at download time) and record in
this file the edition, the download date and how ranks 201–400 were cut. Use it alone — no
patching from other lists — so the ranks in the tables are checkable. Check the licence before
committing the file itself; if in doubt, do as P09 did and cite ranks only.

### D2. One row per lemma, P09's convention

COCA ranks lemma + part of speech; a word may occur twice. P09 listed each word once and split
senses in the *proposed id(s)* column (*work* → `WORK`, `WORK_NOUN`). **Recommendation:** the same,
and say in the checklist's header which convention was used, since it decides the counts.

### D3. Mechanical first, judgement second

1. A throwaway script (scratchpad `.mts` run with `tsx`, per the probe-render habit) imports
   `concepts` from [`concepts/index.ts`](../../../../../packages/backend/src/concepts/index.ts) and
   prints every concept's English `base` (and plural, for nouns), then marks each list lemma
   **seeded** (exact base match, or a known form — *people* of PERSON), **pronoun form**, or
   **unmatched**.
2. Unmatched lemmas are grepped in the English engine sources for **written by the engine**.
3. What is left is read by hand into **left to seed** (a `/seed` row) or **needs the engine first**
   (a construct), applying P09 §1's decisions: D1 (a close seeded concept may cover it), D2 (one
   concept per meaning), D3 (particles become phrasal verbs).

The script is not committed; its output is.

## 1. Deliverable

A checklist in this file's `## Done`, shaped as P09's:

- the status table (*count*, *words*) with the five buckets;
- §2-style tables per role — *rank*, *en*, *proposed id(s)*, *notes* — each row a `/seed` candidate
  with its sense split and any language divergence;

  bundling unrelated ones.

Then, per the localization protocol, the seed rows are catalogued as B tickets and each construct
gets a task file here (P09-E25 onwards), each with a *Today* verified at HEAD.

### Method

1. **Mechanical** (D3.1): a scratchpad `.mts` imported `concepts` from
   `packages/backend/src/concepts/index.ts` and printed every English form of every concept (all
   keys: plurals, verb forms, pronoun forms). There were **672 concepts** at 1229928: 328 nouns, 178
   verbs, 130 adjectives, 31 adverbs and 5 pronouns. 75 lemmas matched a form, among them *anything*
   and *nothing* on SOMETHING's `negative` / `negative_subject` and *much* on VERY's `comparative`.
2. **Engine sources** (D3.2): the 117 unmatched lemmas, plus ten matched ones whose sense was in
   doubt, were grepped as whole words inside string literals in the 51 non-test `.ts` sources of
   `packages/engine/src/languages/en/`.
3. **By hand** (D3.3): every match was checked for its sense. A lemma that matches a seeded concept
   in another sense is *left to seed*, as *hold* and *history* are. Every grep hit was checked for
   being the word (*lead* hit the variable `lead`), and the rest were read into the two last buckets
   under P09 §1 D1–D3.

**Convention (D2)**: one row per lemma, as P09 did. When a lemma's two COCA entries fall in different
buckets, it is counted where its **unbuilt** use puts it, and the other use is named in the row. For
*change*, the verb is seeded and the noun is not, so the lemma is *left to seed*.

**Other lanes of this batch are not counted as present**, since none of them is at 1229928. The ones
that touch this band:

- *Against* is written by the engine as the spatial relation. Its adversarial use is P09-E22's.
- *Job*, *large* and *watch* are covered by a seeded concept (D1). P09-E23's secondary lexemes would
  let the picker find them under their own English word.
- *Hey* waits on the vocative address ([P09-E30](../P09-E30-interjections.md) D1).
- *Onto*, temporal *between*, the Japanese honorific verbs and MOM / DAD as names reach no word of
  the band.

### Defects the probes met, not filed

The probes reproduced five engine defects at 1229928, with seeded words only. No test pins any of them
(grepped). They are **not filed** here, because this batch's lanes are allocating A ids in parallel;
the report hands them to the orchestrator.

1. **A numeral under `ago`**: "two hours ago" writes *il y a **de** deux heures* in French and drops
   the numeral in German, Spanish and Portuguese (*vor Stunden, hace horas, há horas*). Japanese
   writes 日前 for "a day ago"
   ([B80](../../../../localization/B-needs-seed/B80-minute-morning-later-once-often.md) reading 6).
2. **NO_LONGER in an infinitive**, Spanish and Portuguese: *no correr ya no*, *não correr já não*.
   The finite clause is right
   ([B84](../../../../localization/B-needs-seed/B84-stop-wait-die-continue.md) reading 1).
3. **German NO_LONGER before a bare object**: *nicht mehr Gegenstände haben* where *keine Gegenstände
   mehr* is right
   ([B85](../../../../localization/B-needs-seed/B85-pay-provide-spend-win-lose-thank-allow.md)
   reading 4).
4. **A bare plural subject in the Romance languages loses its article**: *chats courent*, *gatti
   corrono*, *gatos corren*
   ([B76](../../../../localization/B-needs-seed/B76-government-and-the-law.md) reading 5). Part of the
